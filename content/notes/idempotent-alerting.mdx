I built a microservice that watches a booked flight and alerts when the fare drops by 10
percent or more. The naive version has an obvious bug: it checks the price on a schedule,
and every check where the price is still down fires another alert. The user gets spammed.

## The fix is state, not cleverness

An alert is not "the price is low right now." It is "the price **crossed** the threshold."
Once you have alerted for a given drop, you record that, and you do not alert again until the
condition resets. The alert becomes **idempotent**: the same qualifying drop produces exactly
one alert, no matter how many times you check.

## Proving it

I created a flight, forced an 18 percent drop, and the alert fired once at the 10 percent
threshold. Then I forced the same drop again: the service returned `alert_triggered: false`
and the alert count stayed at 1. That is the whole feature, and it is the difference between a
tool people keep and a tool people mute.
